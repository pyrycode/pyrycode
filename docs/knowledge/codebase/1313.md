# Ticket #1313 — Probe instrument: prove the sighting carrier's four scalars come from the full-line decode, on an over-cap fixture (offline)

Proves the other half of what #1310 split off: that `finSighting`'s four decoded
scalars (`Subtype`, `IsError`, `TerminalReason`, `StopReason`) come from
`trailScanResult.Trailer` — the decode of the FULL trailer line — and never from a
re-read of `.Line`, which `reachCapCommand` truncates at 512 bytes. The shipped fill
check (`TestFinGatherSightingCarriesTheDecodedScalars`) runs its decoded arm on a
342-byte fixture, under the cap, where both reads give the same answer and the row
passes whichever one the implementation used. This ticket supplies a fixture past
the cap where the two reads genuinely disagree, so the claim is measured rather than
taken on the type's own doc comment. Sibling to #1312 (merged first, proved the
carrier's miss bound the same way). Touches one file, no production `.go` change, no
new type, no call-site change.

## Implementation

- **`TestFinGatherSightingScalarsComeFromTheFullLineDecode`, a standalone test**
  (not a third row on the existing table), mirroring the split #1312 already
  established: the table carries the cheap, non-discriminating claim; a fixture
  that pays for genuine disagreement gets its own test.
- **`finGatherOverCapPad = 200`**, a single-consumer constant declared in its own
  `// --- the over-cap fixture ---` section immediately above the new test (the
  `finWriteTrailerPad` precedent), not in the file's top constants block —
  deliberately, so the two stale-cite corrections below (which point at targets
  *above* line 1471) stay correct in the landed file rather than shifting under
  their own diff.
- `trailPaddedTrailer(200)` renders a 585-byte line. `"terminal_reason"` sits at
  byte 555 — past the 512-byte cap, key and value both cut. `"subtype"` (17),
  `"is_error"` (45) and `"stop_reason"` (348) all sit inside the cap; the first two
  are early enough on the wire order that no pad could ever cut them.
- **The precondition pins the KEY `"terminal_reason"`, never the value
  `"max_turns"`.** The fixture's `subtype` is `error_max_turns`, so the substring
  `"max_turns"` first occurs at byte 34 and survives every cap — a value pin would
  be silently vacuous even on a correct implementation.
- **The contrast is measured, not argued.** A capped copy is `line[:512]` plus a
  truncation marker — JSON cut mid-token — and `json.Unmarshal` validates the whole
  input before filling anything, so a re-read errors and fills a **freshly
  declared** `resultTrailer` with nothing. That is why all four scalars discriminate
  under this pad, not only `terminal_reason`: the alternative read is all-or-nothing
  over the struct, not per-key. Declaring the re-read destination fresh (rather than
  reusing a filled variable) is load-bearing for that to hold.
- **Failure-message constraint, sharper here than anywhere else in the file:** at
  this pad the retained `Line` genuinely carries the modelled payload plus
  `trailNeedle` inside the cap, and no sweep in the file covers this row (the
  no-captured-bytes sweep runs over its own fixture). Messages may name
  `scan.State`, `scan.Detail`, `len(scan.Line)`, and the four scalars; they may
  never render `scan.Line`, `scan`, `scan.Trailer`, or a whole `resultTrailer`.
- **Corrected three stale pointers** inside the edited region only (no file-wide
  sweep): the `#1310` forward-reference at the fill check's doc now names the new
  test by symbol (no line number — the two land in one diff); a C4-row cite
  `(:413-423)` → `(:560-571)`; a "C2 idiom" cite `(:502)` → `(:649)`.

## Patterns established

- **A carrier claim of the shape "field X comes from decode A, never re-derivable
  read B" needs a fixture where A and B disagree**, and the disagreement itself
  must be asserted as a precondition — not just narrated in a comment — so a later
  fixture edit that collapses the disagreement fails loudly rather than leaving the
  row quietly vacuous. Direct continuation of #1312's miss-bound proof, applied to
  a different discriminator (decode source instead of poll timing).
- **When a truncated-input re-read is being ruled out as the source of several
  fields, check whether the parser fails per-field or all-at-once.** `json.Unmarshal`
  is all-or-nothing on a syntax error, which is why every one of the four scalars
  discriminates under this pad even though only one field's bytes were actually cut
  — the other three would otherwise look "decorative" to a reader checking only key
  byte-offsets.

## Lessons learned

- **An AC's "all four scalars discriminate" can be true only via an unstated
  mechanism, and stating the wrong one weakens the row.** The architecture spec's
  first framing risked reading as "the cap reaches each field independently," which
  is false — two of the four keys (`subtype` at byte 17, `is_error` at byte 45) sit
  well inside the cap under any pad in the usable window, and no pad ever reaches
  them directly. What actually makes all four discriminate is that the capped copy
  fails to decode *wholesale* on a syntax error, so a re-read fills nothing rather
  than filling three fields correctly and missing one. When a multi-field claim
  like this is reviewed, check whether the alternative read is per-field or
  all-or-nothing before crediting "all N discriminate" to the fixture alone.
- **A note of the form "a reviewer will otherwise flag this" is worth checking for
  its own error, not just propagating.** An early draft asserted the carrier fill's
  independence from the gate's verdict via an ordering claim ("the fill runs before
  the gate is consulted"), which was backwards — the gate (`:431`) is computed
  *ahead of* the fill (`:442-453`) in the shipped code. The conclusion (the fill
  doesn't depend on the gate) was still correct, but for the right reason: it's
  independence by data dependence (the fill reads only `obs`, never
  `readings.Gate`), not by execution order. The spec's final version states it that
  way and instructs future edits not to paste the ordering phrasing forward.
- **A precondition's floor and the underlying property's floor can differ, and both
  are worth measuring before calling a pin over-strict.** The usable pad window's
  floor of 141 comes from where the `"terminal_reason"` **key** pin stops holding;
  the disagreement property itself (the two reads give different answers) still
  holds one byte earlier, at pad 140, because the key survives but its *value*
  doesn't. That gap is a deliberately stronger precondition, not a bug — pinning
  the bare key is stricter (and simpler to state) than pinning the
  `"terminal_reason":"max_turns"` pair, which would have pushed the floor down to
  129. Silent and correct; worth recording so a future reviewer doesn't "fix" the
  window back down to the property's floor.
