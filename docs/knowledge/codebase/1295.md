# Ticket #1295 — Force the queued-turn-across-`new_session`-rotation ordering deterministically

`TestRelayV2_StreamNewSessionRotatesAndRestartsFresh` (#1137) had failed twice at its M4
milestone with one shape: the rotation succeeds, the daemon accepts the follow-up turn
(`ack #2 received=true`), and zero bytes reach any child for the full 20 s deadline. #1273 had
already ruled out slowness and a second rotation; what remained was "delivered late" vs.
"parked in a silent bound" vs. "erroring persistently." The e2e reproduces at roughly 1-in-N
over weeks, so more running is unbounded work. This ticket forces the suspected ordering
directly at the delivery seam instead of sampling for it: enqueue a turn while the
conversation's turn is running, run the `new_session` rotation underneath it with no step
ordered by a sleep, and check whether the rotation's clear is what releases the parked
delivery.

**Verdict: green.** The forced ordering does **not** reproduce the stall. The test merges as a
permanent guard rather than a repro; no fix was attempted and no production code was touched.

## Implementation

One new file, `cmd/pyry/inbound_deliver_rotation_test.go`, extending the existing
`inbound_deliver_test.go` harness (real `msgqueue.Queue` driving the real `newInboundDeliver`
seam against fakes) with two tests and no `t.Parallel()`:

- **`rotatingWriter`** — a `handlers.TurnWriter` that resolves its target child at *write*
  time, not at resolve time, mirroring `RestartFresh`'s respawn-in-place (the runner identity
  survives the rotation; the child behind `Stdin()` does not). It has no "refuse to write to a
  dead child" branch, deliberately — refusing would mask a misroute behind an error and let
  msgqueue retry it away silently. Three buffered, non-blockingly-signalled channels
  (`activated`, `noChild`, `wrote`) are the test's program-order barriers.
- **`rotatingSessionOwner`** — a mutable `conversationForSession`, replacing the package's
  static `stubBusyResolve` (which can't express a rotation). `rekey` adds the new session id
  and **keeps** the old one, mirroring `RebindSession`'s `SessionHistory` append.
- **`TestInboundDeliver_RotationReleasesHeldTurn_DeliversToFreshChild`** — the forced
  ordering: mark the conversation busy, enqueue a turn, confirm it parks (`assertNoWriteWithin`),
  rekey the binding, tear down the child, fire `clearForSession` keyed to the **new** session
  id, spawn the fresh child, and assert the turn lands there and nowhere else. The hold is the
  **production** `streamTurnHoldTimeout` (15 min) verbatim rather than a shortened one — a
  short hold would convert a stuck-busy defect into a different failure signature (error → 1 Hz
  retry → give-up) than the one on record, which is silent.
- **`TestInboundDeliver_RotationWithoutHold_WritesIntoThePreRotationChild`** — the control:
  same fixture, nil tracker (PTY mode), no hold. Proves the fixture has a reachable path into
  the pre-rotation child.
- AC2 (forced, not sampled) verified with `-race -count=50` on both tests: 50/50 pass, no
  build-failure-reads-as-0/0 ambiguity (the `ok` line was pasted alongside the counts).
- AC3 (demonstrated, not asserted) verified via `go test -overlay`, mutating a scratch copy so
  the worktree stayed byte-clean: **M1** (delete the `waitIdleForDelivery` block) reddens at
  the step-4 `assertNoWriteWithin` — one barrier earlier than the architecture spec predicted,
  because the unheld write lands before the rotation even starts, and it names the
  *pre-rotation* session id, proving the test forces the hold arm specifically. **M2** (replace
  `WriteUserTurn` with `return nil`) reddens on the step-7→8 wake-barrier timeout.

## Patterns established

- **A forced-ordering test at the seam, not a sampled e2e, is the bounded alternative to "run
  it more."** Where a defect's suspected trigger is a specific *ordering* of already-injectable
  steps, drive that ordering directly through channel barriers on the real production call
  graph (real `msgqueue.Queue` + real `newInboundDeliver`, fake `TurnWriter`/session owner)
  rather than adding sleeps or waiting for a low-probability e2e race to fire. `w.activated`
  firing from the statement immediately preceding the hold, on the same goroutine, is what
  makes "the drain is at the hold" a program-order fact rather than a timing guess.
- **Model the seam's resolution timing, not just its final state.** `rotatingWriter` resolving
  its child at write time (not at fixture-construction/resolve time) is what keeps "did the
  turn reach the post-rotation child?" a real question rather than true by construction — the
  same distinction that matters for any fixture standing in for a component whose identity
  survives a respawn while its underlying resource does not.
- **`go test -overlay` answers spec open questions too, not only mutations.** Re-running the
  primary test with step 7 clearing on the *old* session id (instead of the new one specified
  by the design) via an overlay over the test file itself confirmed the `SessionHistory`
  redundancy without a second committed test or a dirty worktree — see "What was decided"
  below.
- **A mutation's message is worth reading for *which* diagnostic it produces, not just for
  red/green.** M2's first run reddened with a message naming only the two "parked" readings; a
  real production drop would have been misdiagnosed as a stuck hold. The message was widened to
  a third clause before the mutation was accepted as evidence. Treat a mutation's output text as
  part of what's being tested, not only its exit status.

## Lessons learned

From the PR body's own **Lessons learned** section
([PR #1297](https://github.com/pyrycode/pyrycode/pull/1297)):

> A mutation can red the *right* test through the *wrong* diagnostic. M2 (drop the write)
> reddened on the wake barrier with a message that named only the two *park* readings — a real
> production drop would have been mis-diagnosed as a stuck hold. The mutation exercise is worth
> running not just for red/green but for whether the message that comes out is the one a future
> operator needs.
>
> `go test -overlay` also answers spec open questions, not only mutations. An overlay over the
> *test* file (not production) answered whether clearing on the pre-rotation id also releases —
> PASS — without a second test, commit, or dirty worktree.
>
> A "forced ordering" test bounds what it can eliminate to exactly the ordering it forces. This
> one forces the non-straggler order and therefore cannot see the straggler-re-mark hypothesis
> at all; recording that limit explicitly is what keeps a green result from reading as "the seam
> is exonerated".

**Two code-review SHOULD FIX findings survived to merge as named, accepted gaps**
([PR #1297 review](https://github.com/pyrycode/pyrycode/pull/1297), decision PASS, 2 SHOULD FIX
— below the three-finding threshold). Both are doc-comment accuracy defects, not defects in the
test's soundness:

1. `inbound_deliver_rotation_test.go:275-277` claims *"The only `setBusy(conv, false)` anywhere
   in this test is step 7's `clearForSession`."* False as stated: the test deliberately takes
   the failing-write arm (step 7 wakes the delivery into a nil child, `WriteUserTurn` returns
   `ErrNoLiveChild`, and `openForDelivery`'s `undo` fires `setBusy(conv, false)` on the drain
   goroutine, once per retry cycle in the step-7→8 window). The conclusion survives — `undo`
   fires strictly downstream of `waitIdleForDelivery` having already returned, so it cannot be
   the release — but the stated evidence is wrong. Suggested fix: narrow to "the only
   `setBusy(conv, false)` that can *release the parked waiter*…".
2. `inbound_deliver_rotation_test.go:410-413` (with the loop at `:379-387`) overreaches: it
   frames the control as making the primary test's "no other child holds the payload" assertion
   non-vacuous "PERMANENTLY," but in the primary test that assertion **is** structurally
   impossible to fail by the time it runs — a write into the pre-rotation child succeeds (the
   writer has no dead-child refusal branch), advances the msgqueue head, and is intercepted by
   an earlier `t.Fatalf` (`:349` or `:371-373`) before the loop at `:379` is ever reached (both
   mutations corroborate: M1 reddens at `:349`, M2 at `:363`, neither at `:383`). What the
   control genuinely proves — narrower, still worth having — is that the *fixture* has a
   reachable path into the pre-rotation child; the file's own "so a future third child cannot
   hide a misroute" framing is the accurate one.

**A future ticket touching this file should fold in both corrections** rather than let them
re-surface as fresh findings — same "SHOULD FIX survives as a named gap" shape #1273 recorded
for this same file family.

## What was decided, and what remains open

Full verbatim command output lives in the architect spec's `## Outcome` section
(`docs/specs/architecture/1295-forced-rotation-delivery-ordering.md`); summarized here:

**Eliminated:** the rotation's `clearForSession` fails to release a delivery parked in
`waitIdleForDelivery`, when the pre-rotation turn's events and the transition arrive in the
forced (non-straggler) order. Forced 50/50, mutation-demonstrated. The seam is not the defect
on this path.

**Open question answered:** clearing on the *pre*-rotation session id also releases the hold
(verified via overlay, not a new test) — the `SessionHistory` redundancy
`transitionClearsTurn`'s doc calls conditional on `notifyTransition` keeping the rebind ahead of
the observer fan-out. It is redundancy, not a second independent guarantee.

**Still open, ranked:**

1. **A straggler pre-rotation event re-marks the conversation after the clear.** The tracker's
   opener feed is asynchronous; a `TextChunk` still buffered in the fan-in when the clear fires
   lands after it and re-marks the conversation busy with a mark no future event can clear —
   matches every feature of the M4 record. This test's forced ordering is precisely the
   non-straggler one and structurally cannot decide this. Filed as
   [#1298](https://github.com/pyrycode/pyrycode/issues/1298), sequenced after #1296.
2. **A dropped exit envelope** (`exitFor`'s non-blocking drop on a full sink) — widens (1)'s
   window. #1296's log capture would show the `stream_turn.exit_sink_full` Warn if it fired.
3. **The persistently-failing error arm** — not separable from a silent park by byte count
   alone. #1296's job (1 Hz `msgqueue: delivery failed, will retry` Warn vs. silence).
4. **The clear-before-`RestartFresh` window** — this test deliberately runs teardown before the
   clear (production runs the clear first) to remove a race rather than decide it; a parked
   turn waking into a still-live doomed child is a window this test does not decide. Argued
   against by the record (that path writes bytes; the shared stdin log showed none) but not
   structurally excluded.
